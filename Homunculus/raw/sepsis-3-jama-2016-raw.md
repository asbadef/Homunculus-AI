# Sepsis-3 — Consensus Definitions (JAMA 2016)

## Definitions

**Sepsis:** "life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to
infection."

**Septic shock:** "a subset of sepsis in which underlying circulatory and cellular/metabolic
abnormalities are profound enough to substantially increase mortality."

## Clinical criteria

**Sepsis:** suspected or documented infection **plus** an acute increase in total SOFA score
of **≥2 points**. Baseline SOFA is assumed to be zero unless pre-existing organ dysfunction
is documented. A SOFA score ≥2 reflects approximately **10% in-hospital mortality** in
general hospital populations with suspected infection — a 2- to 25-fold increased risk of
death compared with SOFA <2.

**Septic shock:** sepsis **plus both** of:
- vasopressor therapy required to maintain **MAP ≥65 mmHg**, and
- **serum lactate >2 mmol/L (>18 mg/dL)** despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

Hospital mortality with both criteria present: **>40%**.

## qSOFA (quick SOFA)

A bedside prompt for possible sepsis outside the ICU. **≥2 of 3**:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Respiratory rate | ≥22/min |
| Systolic blood pressure | ≤100 mmHg |
| Altered mentation | GCS <15 |

Predictive validity outside the ICU: **AUROC 0.81** (95% CI 0.80–0.82) for poor outcome
(prolonged ICU stay or hospital death).

## SOFA score

| Organ system | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Respiration** PaO₂/FiO₂, mmHg | ≥400 | <400 | <300 | <200 + respiratory support | <100 + respiratory support |
| **Coagulation** platelets ×10³/µL | ≥150 | <150 | <100 | <50 | <20 |
| **Liver** bilirubin mg/dL | <1.2 | 1.2–1.9 | 2.0–5.9 | 6.0–11.9 | >12.0 |
| **Cardiovascular** | MAP ≥70 mmHg | MAP <70 mmHg | dopamine <5 *or* dobutamine any dose | dopamine 5.1–15 *or* epinephrine ≤0.1 *or* norepinephrine ≤0.1 | dopamine >15 *or* epinephrine >0.1 *or* norepinephrine >0.1 |
| **CNS** GCS | 15 | 13–14 | 10–12 | 6–9 | <6 |
| **Renal** creatinine mg/dL | <1.2 | 1.2–1.9 | 2.0–3.4 | 3.5–4.9 | >5.0 |
| **Renal** urine output | — | — | <500 mL/d | — | <200 mL/d |

Catecholamine doses in µg/kg/min for ≥1 hour.

## Mortality figures reported

| Cohort / condition | Hospital mortality |
|---|---|
| SOFA ≥2 (general hospital, suspected infection) | ~10% |
| Hyperlactataemia alone (>2 mmol/L), no vasopressors | 25.7% |
| Fluid-resistant hypotension on vasopressors, lactate ≤2 | 30.1% |
| Septic shock (vasopressors + lactate >2), SSC registry | 42.3% |
| Septic shock, lactate >4 mmol/L, SSC registry | 49.7% |
| Septic shock, UPMC cohort (n=315) | 54% |
| Septic shock, Kaiser Permanente N. California (n=8,051) | 35% |

## Stated limitations

1. **No gold-standard diagnostic test exists.** Sepsis is identified by a constellation of
   clinical signs in a patient with suspected infection.
2. **SOFA requires laboratory data** (PaO₂, platelets, creatinine, bilirubin) and "may not
   promptly capture dysfunction in individual organ systems."
3. **The cardiovascular sub-score can be affected by iatrogenic interventions** — it partly
   measures the treatment, not only the patient.
4. **Derivation was largely from US databases.** The task force "strongly encourages
   prospective validation in multiple US and non-US health care settings."
5. **Lactate is not universally available**, especially in developing countries. The septic
   shock lactate criterion passed with only 13/18 (72.2%) of votes and "warrants revisiting."
6. **Adults only.** The task force "recognizes the need to develop similar updated
   definitions for pediatric populations."
7. **"Neither qSOFA nor SOFA is intended to be a stand-alone definition of sepsis."**
