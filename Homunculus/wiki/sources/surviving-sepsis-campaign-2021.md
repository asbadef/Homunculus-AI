# Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 (SUPERSEDED)

**What it is.** The previous edition of the international sepsis management guideline (Evans,
Rhodes, Alhazzani, et al., *Intensive Care Med* 2021;47(11):1181–1247). **Superseded by**
[[surviving-sepsis-campaign-2026]].

**Authority: high for its era — do not cite as current practice.** It is retained because the
*deltas* between 2021 and 2026 are clinically informative, and because any historical data,
protocol, or model trained on 2021-era practice must remain auditable against the guideline
that was actually in force at the time. This is the "supersede, don't overwrite" rule in
action.

## Key claims it supports (historical)

- **Strong recommendation AGAINST qSOFA as a single screening tool** vs SIRS/NEWS/MEWS —
  moderate quality. Rationale: qSOFA sensitivity was only **24%** in the derivation study.
  → [[sepsis-screening-tools]], [[qsofa]]
- **≥30 mL/kg crystalloid within 3 hours** — weak, low quality (2026 keeps the number,
  still conditional). → [[initial-resuscitation-sepsis]]
- **MAP target 65 mmHg** — strong, moderate quality; higher targets gave no mortality benefit
  and more atrial fibrillation. → [[map-target-sepsis]]
- **Antimicrobials within 1 hour for septic shock** — strong. **Within 3 hours for sepsis
  without shock** — weak, very low quality. → [[antimicrobial-timing-sepsis]]
- **Source control within 6–12 hours** — Best Practice Statement.
  → [[source-control-sepsis]]

## What changed in 2026 — the deltas worth knowing

| Topic | 2021 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Antimicrobials, sepsis **without** shock | within **3 h**, *weak* | within **1 h**, ***strong*** |
| MAP target, age ≥65 | not addressed | **60–65 mmHg**, conditional |
| qSOFA for screening | strong **against** as sole tool | strong **for** NEWS/NEWS2/MEWS/SIRS **over** qSOFA |
| Fluid removal after resuscitation | not addressed | **active fluid removal**, conditional |
| Source control | 6–12 h (best practice statement) | ideally **6 h**, conditional |

The antimicrobial change is the most consequential for an alerting system: the window in which
an alert is clinically useful for non-shock sepsis **narrowed from three hours to one**. An
alert that arrives at 90 minutes was compliant with 2021 and is late under 2026. See
[[antimicrobial-timing-sepsis]].
