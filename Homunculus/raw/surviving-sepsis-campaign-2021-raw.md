# Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2021 — Adult Guidelines (SUPERSEDED)

Evans L, Rhodes A, Alhazzani W, et al. *Intensive Care Med.* 2021;47(11):1181–1247.

## Key recommendations captured

### Sepsis screening

- **Rec 2: Strong recommendation AGAINST using qSOFA** compared with SIRS, NEWS, or MEWS as a
  **single** screening tool — moderate-quality evidence.
  - Rationale given: qSOFA is "more specific but less sensitive" than SIRS. Sensitivity in the
    original derivation study was only **24%**, despite qSOFA-positive patients accounting for
    70% of poor outcomes.

### Initial resuscitation

- **Rec 5: At least 30 mL/kg IV crystalloid within the first 3 hours** for sepsis-induced
  hypoperfusion or septic shock — **weak recommendation, low-quality evidence**. Referenced to
  ideal body weight.
- **Rec 7: Guide resuscitation to decrease serum lactate** in patients with elevated lactate —
  **weak, low-quality evidence**. Lactate must be "interpreted considering the clinical context
  and other causes."

### Haemodynamics

- **Rec 9: Initial MAP target 65 mmHg** over higher targets — **strong, moderate-quality
  evidence**. Higher targets showed no mortality benefit and increased atrial fibrillation risk.

### Antimicrobials

- **Rec 12: Septic shock — antimicrobials immediately, ideally within 1 hour of recognition** —
  **strong**, low-quality evidence.
- **Rec 14: Sepsis without shock — antimicrobials within 3 hours** from first recognition,
  following rapid assessment — **weak, very low-quality evidence**.

### Source control

- **Rec 27: Best Practice Statement** — rapidly identify an anatomical diagnosis of infection
  requiring emergent source control, and implement it "as soon as medically and logistically
  practical." Supporting evidence cited suggests source control **within 6–12 hours** is
  advantageous.
