# Neonatal Sepsis — Comprehensive Review (Antibiotics 2024)

## No consensus definition exists

**"No clear consensus definition for sepsis in neonates exists."** Definitional variability
across the literature causes "ambiguity in the interpretation, comparison, and generalization
of the results."

This is the central fact of the NICU sepsis problem, and it stands in contrast to adults
([[sepsis-3-jama-2016]]) and older children ([[phoenix-criteria-jama-2024]]), both of which
now have consensus organ-dysfunction-based definitions. Neonates have neither.

## Early-onset vs late-onset sepsis

| | Early-onset (EOS) | Late-onset (LOS) |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Within first 7 days of life (CDC); for infants hospitalised from birth, limited to the **first 72 hours**, beyond which nosocomial pathogens take over | Beyond **3–7 days** of life |
| **Acquisition** | Perinatal / maternal | Hospital environment or community |
| **Predominant organisms** | Group B *Streptococcus* (term); *E. coli* (preterm/VLBW). Together **two-thirds of all EOS** | Gram-positive in high-income countries (coagulase-negative staphylococci); Gram-negative in low/lower-middle-income countries. ***Candida* spp. are the third most common cause of LOS in VLBW infants** |

## Diagnostic challenges

- **Culture-negative sepsis is common.** Blood culture is the gold standard, yet a negative
  culture "does not rule out sepsis." Reported rates of culture-negative/suspected sepsis
  "vary widely in the literature."
- **Blood volume limits culture sensitivity.** Optimal detection needs **≥1 mL of blood** — a
  meaningful volume in a neonate. Low-level bacteraemia compounds the problem.
- **Signs are non-specific** and "often overlap with other neonatal diseases" such as
  respiratory distress syndrome and meconium aspiration.

## Clinical presentation — and how it differs from adults

- **Temperature instability**, not fever: **hyperthermia** usually in term neonates,
  **hypothermia** most often in preterm neonates.
- **Respiratory:** tachypnoea, grunting, **apnoea**, cyanosis, nasal flaring, accessory
  muscle use.
- **Cardiovascular:** **tachycardia is an early sign**. Hypotension and poor perfusion
  (delayed capillary refill, pallor, mottling, cool extremities) "tend to arise later."
  Critically: **"Abnormal heart rates with decreased variability and transient decelerations
  are found to precede the diagnosis of LOS by hours, even days."**
- **Neurological:** lethargy, irritability, high-pitched crying, hypotonia, blunted reflexes,
  poor feeding, rarely seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** feeding intolerance, vomiting, diarrhoea, jaundice, abdominal
  distension, hepatosplenomegaly.

## Risk factors

**Prematurity is the most significant factor** correlated with both EOS and LOS — immune
immaturity, low transplacental maternal IgG (acquired mainly in the second half of pregnancy),
and prolonged hospital stay.

- **Maternal:** GBS rectovaginal colonisation, prolonged rupture of membranes (**≥18 hours**),
  intrapartum fever, chorioamnionitis.
- **Neonatal:** male sex, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, traumatic delivery, perinatal
  asphyxia, need for resuscitation or intubation at birth, congenital anomalies.
- **Iatrogenic (LOS):** central venous catheters, prolonged IV access, parenteral nutrition,
  invasive mechanical ventilation, prolonged hospitalisation.

## Biomarkers

| Marker | Kinetics | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **CRP** | Rises 8–12 h, peaks 24–48 h | "Unacceptably low sensitivity within the first 24 h." Two normal values (8–24 h after birth, then 24 h later) give **NPV 99.7%** — useful to rule *out*, not to rule *in*. Single values must not guide decisions |
| **Procalcitonin** | Rises in 2–4 h, peaks 6–12 h | Slightly better early sensitivity than CRP, slightly lower specificity |
| **Presepsin (sCD14)** | Rises after 2 h, peaks at 3 h | Highest reported accuracy: **88.9% sensitivity, 85.7% specificity** for EOS |
| **I:T neutrophil ratio** | >0.22 preterm, >0.27 term | More specific, good predictive value, but elevated in **25–50% of uninfected infants** |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | — | Frequent but non-specific; a **late** sign |

**Neither CRP nor PCT is recommended as a single marker of infection.** Both are elevated by
fetal hypoxia, delivery stress, RDS, meconium aspiration, IVH, surgery, and pneumothorax.

## Continuous monitoring and machine learning

**HeRO score (Heart Rate Observation), developed 2005.** A non-invasive, continuous monitoring
method that analyses heart rate deviations to provide "early indications of sepsis prior to any
symptoms." Applicable to VLBW, preterm and full-term infants in the NICU. The review notes that
"incorporation with other newer and different risk assessment tools improves the outcome."

**Machine learning / AI:** research "has been focusing on the rapidly evolving machine learning
modeling and artificial intelligence methods, which take readily available individualized
patient parameters into account" — but these are **not yet in clinical application**. Likewise
"omics"-based strategies (metabolomics, proteomics, genomics) are "promising but not yet ready
for clinical application."
